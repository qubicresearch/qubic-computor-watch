Tracing all 676 computor seats

A step-by-step account of how a "self-funded" computor bond turns out, almost every time, to be neither self-funded nor independent — with the real transaction hashes, ticks, and amounts behind every hop.

Compiled from live on-chain data, rpc.qubic.org and bob.qubic.global · epochs 226–229 · last updated 2026-09-08

Executive summary

Every current Qubic computor identity is funded by exactly one of three things: a known pool wallet, the network's own 1,000,000 QU candidacy bond, or — it turns out — a wallet that only looks unrelated to anything until you check one or two hops further. Checking only an identity's first-ever transaction, the way any casual audit would, classifies roughly 30–60% of every epoch's computors as "self-funded." That classification is almost entirely wrong.

Once you check what a "self-funded" identity does after that first transaction — a real secondary funding event, and a real payout it forwards onward — the true picture for epoch 228 is that all 676 computor seats trace back to one of exactly two operator networks, or a smaller identified "other" bucket — zero remain genuinely unexplained. Nothing that looks like broad, independent participation survives contact with the actual transaction graph. (Epochs 226 and 227 are fully resolved to zero unexplained too — see the full epoch tables.)

The three funding layers

Once you look past the first transaction, three distinct, real patterns appear, layered on top of each other:

Layer 1 — direct seeding

A known operator wallet (TKOG, YNRE, the MLAB family, the JETSKI-branded wallets, ZTQM, TMNN) funds a brand-new identity directly, usually with a round amount — 3,000,000, 20,000,000, or 50,000,000 QU depending on the wallet and era. This is the only layer a simple first-transaction check actually catches.

Layer 2 — the 9,000,000 QU secondary funding

An identity that looks self-funded on its first transaction (a plain 1,000,000 QU bond, source unrecognized) very often receives a second, separate payment of exactly 9,000,000 QU some time later — not from a known hub, but from one of a small, closed set of wallets:

Layer 3 — the real epoch payout, forwarded onward within minutes

This is the layer that actually resolves most of the remaining mystery, and it is not unique to one family — both networks show the identical structural pattern. A computor that survives long enough to earn a real epoch payout receives that payout directly from the network's own protocol address, and — almost without exception — forwards the exact same amount onward within minutes, to a known collection wallet.

qli side

computor→ JBWF / VAQA / KOYO / IHDR / PCUTRGWY / KDHVRGXD / LHDR / DMRK→ QLINAMIOESD...

The original eight qli hubs each show the identical structural signature — dozens of distinct computors paying in the same real amount (268,701,925 QU was the signature for the epoch this was first documented in; 537,403,847 QU and others recur in later epochs), balance drained to zero, forwarding onward to the same consolidation wallet. See the hub catalog below for the exact numbers behind each.

MinerLab/JetSki side — the same pattern

computor→ MLABG / MLABN / MLABD / MLABR / MLABZ / YNRE / ZTQM

The same structural signature shows up here too: a computor receives its real epoch payout directly from the protocol address, then forwards almost the exact same amount, within the same window, to whichever MLAB-family hub is collecting that week — the rotating cast, not one fixed address, is the point. Confirmed on many computors independently (see the hub catalog and the live tracer above), and from the receiving side too: BCGI, LIRS, and UYIG (see the network map) are each fed directly and repeatedly by multiple confirmed MLAB wallets at once.

How the payout comes back out to individual miners

qli side: QLINAMIOESD... and QLIFODMBOSRW... pay individual computors via a single QUTIL SendToManyV1 contract call — one transaction, decoded from its raw input data into up to 25 distinct recipient payments (see the live tracer above, and the KNOWN-wallet notes on QLINAM/QLIF/VCHM/QOBK below).

MinerLab/JetSki side: the three JETSKI-branded payout wallets (JETSKIYUBUPF, JETSKIQRDEIV, JETSKIMZKTSL) pay individual computors with plain, separate transfers — one transaction per recipient, no contract call. Real sample from JETSKIYUBUPF's own outgoing history: 155,364,660 / 182,828,110 / 239,324,350 / 155,364,660 / 171,058,060 QU to five different addresses across ~150 real ticks. Same real function (paying individual miners), different real transaction shape.

Update, 2026-09-07: the "eight hubs" figure for the qli side describes what had been confirmed when this section was first written. Since then, this investigation has traced a much larger network on both sides — dozens more collection points, several more QLI-vanity-prefixed wallets, a QUTIL SendToManyV1 payout mechanism, and multiple confirmed MinerLab/JetSki collection wallets (BCGI, LIRS, UYIG, IXAY) fed directly by the MLAB family — see the full network map for the current picture.

Why MinerLab and JetSki can't be separated

"MinerLab" and "JetSki" are two labels a third-party heuristic service applies to computor operators. Every piece of direct evidence found this investigation says they are one operation using two brand names, not two operations:

  • Shared clearinghouse. LKBOPOUKGSYT receives directly from MLAB-family wallets and forwards directly to JETSKI-branded wallets, in the same tick window, repeatedly. Money from one "brand" physically passes through the same wallet as money going to the other.
  • Shared satellite funders. RPQDNLQUXXZ (above) and the eleven 9,000,000-QU funder wallets all show direct lineage to confirmed MinerLab/JetSki wallets while operating under no brand name of their own.
What this doesn't claim

This is evidence of one coordinated money-flow network operating under two labels — it is not evidence about which entity, if either, "owns" the other, and no claim is made about legal structure or corporate relationship. The fund flow is direct and repeated; the org chart behind it is not something on-chain data can show.

What we can't say

  • Epoch 229 isn't in the table above yet. Computors don't receive their real epoch revenue until roughly two days into the following epoch — so a current or recent epoch can't be fully traced by this method until that payout has actually landed.
  • An identity marked "unexplained" at any point in this investigation was never proven independent — it just meant the checks run so far (direct seeding, the 9M signature, the 268M qli-hub payout, or the later broader 100M–999M known-hub sweep) hadn't found a match yet. Every epoch's "unexplained" bucket in this investigation has, so far, resolved to zero once checked thoroughly enough — a pattern worth keeping in mind for any future epoch that still shows one.
  • LHDR and DMRK, the two largest qli collection hubs (5,982 and 10,000+ transactions), are not exhaustively mapped, but their top outgoing flows are confirmed: both feed a new QLI-branded wallet (22.2B QU each), and both feed directly into this session's newly-discovered cluster — LHDR to YHPK, DMRK to UUSJ and CWKWQ — tying the legacy hubs and the new cluster into one confirmed network. Their full sender lists (thousands of individual computor payments each) remain uncatalogued.
  • No claim is made about who controls any of these wallets as a legal or personal matter. Everything above is a description of fund flow between addresses, verifiable by anyone against the same public API.
  • No Gini coefficient, HHI, or Nakamoto coefficient across the 676 seats yet. The CCF vote clustering does prove real internal sub-structure inside the mj/qli fund-flow families — a 105-address bloc (proposals 67/73) and 6 separate non-overlapping clusters in proposal 2 (1 qli, 5 mj) — so neither "mj" nor "qli" is a single entity. But those blocs were only identified from computors who happened to vote on two specific proposals: they don't cover all 676 seats, haven't been checked for stability across other proposals or epochs, and the 105-bloc hasn't been cross-checked against the proposal-2 clusters to confirm whether it's the same group or a different one. A concentration index requires every seat assigned to one real entity in a complete, non-overlapping partition — this investigation has solid partial evidence of real entities, not yet that complete assignment.
